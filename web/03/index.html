Watch an AI agent think

Pick a tool, use it, answer

Imagine you ask a friend a question. Before answering, they pause: "Do I know this, or do I need my calculator, or should I look it up?" Then they do that — and then they answer.

That pause is what people mean when they call an AI an agent. It doesn't just answer. It first decides how to answer, sometimes using a tool along the way.

The agent on this page has two tools: a calculator, and a small book of 6 trivia facts. It picks a tool with a simple rule, not real thinking — so you can see every step, including the wrong turns.

6 examples: which tool did it pick?

Each example shows a question, then every step the agent took.

[THINKING] deciding what to do · [TOOL CALL] the tool it picked, and what it typed in · [TOOL RESULT] what the tool gave back · [FINAL ANSWER] what you would see

  1. You ask: what is 12 times 4

    [THINKING] Checking whether this needs a tool...
[TOOL CALL] calculator("12 * 4")
[TOOL RESULT] 48
[FINAL ANSWER] 12 * 4 = 48

    ✅ Right tool. It saw numbers and the word "times", so it used the calculator.

  2. You ask: what is twelve times four

    [THINKING] Checking whether this needs a tool...
[THINKING] No tool matches — not math, not one of my trivia topics.
[FINAL ANSWER] I can only do math (e.g. "12 * 4") or answer a handful of trivia questions. Try one of those!

    ❌ No tool. It's the same question, with the numbers written as words. The rule only spots digits like 12, so it gives up.

  3. You ask: what is the fastest land animal

    [THINKING] Checking whether this needs a tool...
[TOOL CALL] search_trivia("what is the fastest land animal")
[TOOL RESULT] The cheetah, which can sprint up to about 100-120 km/h (60-75 mph) in short bursts.
[FINAL ANSWER] The cheetah, which can sprint up to about 100-120 km/h (60-75 mph) in short bursts.

    ✅ Right tool. The question matches one of its 6 trivia facts, so it looked it up.

  4. You ask: how many moons does saturn have

    [THINKING] Checking whether this needs a tool...
[TOOL CALL] search_trivia("how many moons does saturn have")
[TOOL RESULT] Jupiter has over 95 confirmed moons, and astronomers keep finding more. Only Saturn has more.
[FINAL ANSWER] Jupiter has over 95 confirmed moons, and astronomers keep finding more. Only Saturn has more.

    ❌ Wrong answer, said with confidence. You asked about Saturn, but it has no Saturn fact. "How many moons … have" matches its Jupiter fact closely enough, so it answers about Jupiter instead.

  5. You ask: tell me a joke

    [THINKING] Checking whether this needs a tool...
[THINKING] No tool matches — not math, not one of my trivia topics.
[FINAL ANSWER] I can only do math (e.g. "12 * 4") or answer a handful of trivia questions. Try one of those!

    ✅ Honest. None of its tools can tell a joke, so it says so instead of making something up.

  6. You ask: I have 3 apples and eat 1, how many are left?

    [THINKING] Checking whether this needs a tool...
[TOOL CALL] search_trivia("I have 3 apples and eat 1, how many are left?")
[TOOL RESULT] Jupiter has over 95 confirmed moons, and astronomers keep finding more. Only Saturn has more.
[FINAL ANSWER] Jupiter has over 95 confirmed moons, and astronomers keep finding more. Only Saturn has more.

    ❌ Wrong tool. A person sees an easy sum, but there's no "minus" or "−" for the calculator rule to spot. "How many … have" looks like the Jupiter question, so you get a fact about moons.

The lesson: an agent is only as good as the way it picks its tools. This one follows a simple rule, so it misses questions written a little differently — and can pick the wrong tool without noticing. Real AI agents let the AI itself decide, which is much smarter, but they can still pick wrong.
