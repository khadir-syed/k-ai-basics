Ask AI better

Same AI, clearer question, better answer

Imagine you ask a friend: "Get me a snack." They come back with a whole bag of crisps, three biscuits and a banana. Not wrong — just not what you wanted.

Now you say: "Get me one green apple from the fruit bowl, cut into four slices, please." This time you get exactly that. Your friend didn't get smarter. You got clearer.

AI works the same way. The message you type is called a prompt, and small changes to it — saying what exactly, how long, what shape and who it's for — change the answer a lot.

4 examples: a vague question vs a clear one

We asked a real AI each question and saved its answers. Look at how long each answer is — and whether it's what you'd actually want.

These are real answers from the AI model gpt-oss-120b, saved on 23 September 2026. Ask today and the words will differ, but the pattern usually holds.

AI tools write their answers with formatting symbols, like ## for a heading or | for a table. Chat apps turn those into headings and tables — and so does this page.

  1. 1. Too vague

    ❌ Vague: Write about dogs

    ✅ Clear: Write a 3-sentence, upbeat product description for a dog leash, aimed at first-time dog owners.

    What changed:

    • Specific: a dog leash, not "dogs" in general
    • Format: a product description
    • Limit: 3 sentences, so it can't ramble
    • Audience: first-time dog owners, so it picks the right words

    Checklist score: 0/5 → 4/5

  2. 2. No format

    ❌ Vague: Tell me about exercise

    ✅ Clear: Give me 3 bullet points, one line each, on easy ways a busy office worker can exercise at home.

    What changed:

    • Format: bullet points
    • Limit: 3 points, one line each
    • Specific: easy exercise at home, not all of exercise
    • Audience: a busy office worker

    Checklist score: 0/5 → 4/5

  3. 3. No limits

    ❌ Vague: Write an email asking for a day off

    ✅ Clear: Write a polite email to my manager asking for a day off on Friday 3 October. Keep it under 80 words.

    What changed:

    • Limit: under 80 words
    • Specific: which day, and a polite tone
    • Audience: my manager

    Checklist score: 1/5 → 4/5

  4. 4. No role or audience

    ❌ Vague: Explain taxes

    ✅ Clear: You are a friendly teacher. Explain what taxes are to a 10-year-old in 4 short sentences, using a lemonade stand as the example.

    What changed:

    • Role: a friendly teacher
    • Audience: a 10-year-old
    • Limit: 4 short sentences
    • Specific: a lemonade stand example

    Checklist score: 0/5 → 4/5

Some of the long answers stop mid-sentence: the AI was still going when it reached the length limit we gave it.

The lesson: a vague question gets a long answer about everything. Say what exactly, what shape, how long and who it's for — and you get what you wanted.
