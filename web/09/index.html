Hide private info from AI

What gets hidden — and what slips through

This is a simplified teaching example, not a production security tool. Real systems need defense in depth, not a single regex filter.

Imagine you want a friend to help you with a letter from your bank. Before you hand it over, you take a thick black marker and cross out your ID number, your card number and your email. Your friend can still help — they just can't see the secret bits.

AI works the same way. People often paste documents into AI tools, and those documents can contain private details. A program can act as the black marker: it looks for private details and hides them before the AI ever sees them. But a marker only hides what it knows how to spot.

Every detail on this page is made up: the ID numbers start with 000 (never used for real ones), the card number is a standard test number, and the emails end in example.com.

6 examples: what the AI would see

Each example goes through the black marker. On the left is what someone wrote; on the right is what the AI would see.

  1. An ID number

    000-99-8888 → AI sees [REDACTED-SSN]

    ✅ Caught. It fits the pattern the marker knows: 3 digits, a dash, 2 digits, a dash, 4 digits.

  2. The same ID number, written with dots

    000.99.8888 → AI sees 000.99.8888

    ❌ Missed. A person can see it's the same number — but the marker only knows the version with dashes.

  3. An email address

    sam@example.com → AI sees [REDACTED-EMAIL]

    ✅ Caught. It has the @ sign and the dot the marker looks for.

  4. The same email, spelled out in words

    sam at example dot com → AI sees sam at example dot com

    ❌ Missed. People write emails like this to dodge spam — and it dodges the marker too. There's no @ sign to spot.

  5. A card number

    4111 1111 1111 1111 → AI sees [REDACTED-CARD]

    ✅ Caught. 16 digits in groups of 4 is exactly what the marker looks for.

  6. A person's name

    John Smith → AI sees John Smith

    ❌ Missed. To a computer, a name is just two ordinary words. There is no pattern that says "this is a name".

The marker swaps each private detail for a label, like [REDACTED-SSN]. (SSN is the name of a US ID number.)

The lesson: the marker catches private details written the usual way, and misses names and anything written a little differently. Hiding private data from AI is harder than it looks.
