A young shepherd boy was bored watching sheep on the hillside all day, so he
decided to play a trick on the villagers below. He cried out at the top of
his lungs that a wolf was attacking the sheep.

The villagers heard his cries and came running up the hill with sticks and
tools to chase the wolf away. But when they arrived, there was no wolf at
all, only the shepherd boy laughing at how easily he had fooled them.

A few days later, the bored shepherd boy played the same trick again,
shouting that a wolf was attacking his flock. Once more the villagers dropped
what they were doing and rushed up the hill to help, only to find the boy
laughing at them a second time.

Then one evening a real wolf actually appeared and began attacking the
sheep. The shepherd boy cried out as loudly as he could for help, but this
time none of the villagers believed him, since he had lied to them twice
before.

No one came to help, and the wolf scattered the flock. The shepherd boy
learned a hard lesson that evening: once you have lied, people stop believing
you even when you are telling the truth.
